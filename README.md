# Why Atlas Exists

Spatial Intelligence is emerging as one of the defining engineering disciplines of the coming decades. From XR and wearable computing to robotics, autonomous systems, mapping, and intelligent devices, an increasing number of technologies rely on machines that can perceive, understand, and reason about the physical world.

Building these systems requires far more than mastering individual algorithms or frameworks. It demands a rare combination of mathematical intuition, software engineering, systems thinking, and engineering judgment.

Atlas exists as a long-term engineering laboratory dedicated to developing those capabilities through deliberate practice, production-quality software, and continuous experimentation.

Its purpose is not simply to study Spatial Intelligence, but to become the kind of engineer capable of designing, building, and evolving the systems that will define this field.

---

# Philosophy

Atlas is guided by a simple belief:

**Engineering excellence is built through deliberate practice, first-principles thinking, and continuous refinement.**

Learning is not measured by the number of completed courses, but by the ability to understand complex systems, make sound engineering decisions, and build reliable software that solves meaningful problems.

Every project in Atlas exists to answer an engineering question. Every experiment should leave behind reusable knowledge. Every sprint should improve not only technical skills, but also engineering judgment.

# Engineering Principles

## Engineering Decisions

Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) document important engineering decisions taken throughout Atlas.

See `docs/adr`.

## Learn from First Principles

Understand why systems work before learning how they are implemented.

## Build Professionally

Treat every project as production-quality software.

## Think in Systems

Connect mathematics, algorithms, software engineering, and product constraints into a coherent whole.

## Optimize for Engineering Judgment

Prioritize sound decisions over clever implementations or excessive complexity.

## Create Reusable Knowledge

Every project, note, experiment, and document should become an asset for future work.

## Stay Curious

Follow questions, not trends. Technologies evolve; first principles endure.

## Iterate Relentlessly

Atlas is never finished. Every sprint is an opportunity to refine both the laboratory and the engineer behind it.
